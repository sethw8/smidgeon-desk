Recording your voice

What we're asking

We'd like your voice to be the narrator of Smidgeon Scholars, and all we need from you is about 90 seconds of reading. From that one recording, a voice tool makes every narrator line in your voice, including new stories later, without you recording each one.

  • Your yes comes first. Nothing is made until you agree, and you can say no or change your mind at any time. If you stop, your recording and the cloned voice are deleted.
  • It's only for Smidgeon Scholars. Your voice isn't used for anything else, shared, or sold.
  • You approve what ships. You hear sample lines before anything goes in the app, and you can ask for any line to be redone or recorded by you instead.
  • Smidgeon keeps his own voice. Only the narrator, the warm grown-up voice, becomes yours.

How to record

A phone is plenty; what matters most is a quiet room.

  1. Pick a small, soft room: a bedroom or a closet with clothes works better than a kitchen. Turn off fans, heaters and music.
  2. Use Voice Memos (iPhone) or any recorder app. Hold the phone about a hand's width from your mouth, a little to the side so breaths don't pop.
  3. Read it the way you'd read to a child on your lap: warm, unhurried, smiling where it fits. Don't perform; your natural teaching voice is what we want.
  4. Mistakes are fine. Pause, then start that sentence again. We'll cut it.
  5. Keep going for the whole script in one take if you can (about 90 seconds), then listen back once: no background noise, no clipping.
  6. Send the file as it comes from the app (an .m4a is perfect) to Seth by email or AirDrop.

The script (about 90 seconds)

Read the paragraphs in order, with a short breath between them. The notes in brackets are the feeling to read with; don't say them aloud.

1. Welcome(warm, like greeting a child at the door)

Hi there, friend. Welcome to Smidgeon Scholars. Smidgeon is a small pigeon with very big feelings. Some days are sunny, and some days are bumpy. Today, we'll play a few little games together, and every game helps our tree grow a brand-new branch.

2. A story(storytelling, gently building)

Smidgeon built a tower of blocks. One, two, three, four, five, so tall! Then his wing bumped it, and crash! Down it came. His cheeks got hot. His feathers puffed up. That's steam. Is it a Bummer, a Problem, or a Disaster? What do you think?

3. Calm(slow and soft, almost a whisper)

Let's breathe together. Smell the flower, slowly in. Now blow out the candle, slowly out. One more time. In… and out. There. Can you feel it getting quieter inside?

4. Cheering(bright and proud)

You did it! You named the feeling, and you found your way back to still. Pip and Wren are cheering. Hooray for you!

5. For the grown-up(your normal speaking voice, kind and direct)

Grown-ups, here's one thing to try tonight. Sit close, at their level, and name one feeling you noticed today. You don't have to fix it. You just have to be there for it.

What happens next

  1. Seth sends you about 10 sample lines from the app in your cloned voice, within a day or two of getting your recording.
  2. You listen and tell us what sounds right and what doesn't. If it isn't you yet, we can try a second recording.
  3. Once you're happy, every narrator line is remade in your voice and reaches families as an app update, with no App Store wait.
  4. New stories from then on are narrated in your voice too, and you'll hear each new batch before it goes out.
